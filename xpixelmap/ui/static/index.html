How it works

Fixing a hot pixel, in plain words

A hot pixel is one tiny spot on the sensor that leaks a little charge, so it shows up as a bright dot in dark parts of a photo. This tool does what Fujifilm's factory pixel mapping does: it adds that spot to the camera's own list of faulty pixels, so the camera can leave it out of every photo. It does not change the sensor, the firmware or your photos.

1 What a hot pixel is

Your camera's sensor is a grid of millions of tiny light sensors, called photosites. Each one turns light into an electrical signal. A normal photosite reads almost nothing in the dark. A hot pixel leaks a little charge anyway, so it reads brighter than its neighbours even when no light reaches it.

The result is a small bright dot. It stays in the same place from photo to photo, usually becomes more obvious at higher ISO settings and in long exposures, and is easiest to see in the dark parts of a picture.

A hot pixel is not dust. Dust sits on the sensor cover, can move when you clean the sensor, and often looks soft or dark. A hot pixel is a fixed coordinate on the sensor, and cleaning will not remove it. A dead pixel is the opposite: it reads too low, so it can look like a dark dot.

The important part: the camera already knows how to hide a pixel it has been told about. The missing piece on the X-T1 is a menu for telling it.

2 What the camera already does

Inside the camera is a small configuration image. Among other things, it holds lists of defective-pixel coordinates. When the camera saves a photo, it checks those lists and replaces each listed pixel with a value built from the pixels around it. That is Fujifilm's own pixel mapping, and it is why a repaired camera does not simply show a black hole where the bad pixel was.

At the factory, the camera is pointed at a dark scene, the worst pixels are measured, and their coordinates are written into those lists. Some later Fujifilm bodies have a Pixel Mapping menu that repeats this at home. The X-T1 does not, so Fujifilm's answer is a paid service visit.

3 What this tool changes

X Pixel Map reads that configuration image from your own camera, checks it, and builds a new copy with only the defect tables edited. It then writes the whole image back, reads it back, and compares it byte for byte. Nothing else in the image is allowed to differ from your own backup.

4 How a session works

Connect→Save settings→ Find→Review→ Build→Write→ Check
  1. Connect and save your menu settings. The camera is read in normal mode first. Service mode resets the clock and menu settings, so the tool saves them and puts them back at the end.
  2. Find. The tool takes five dark frames with the lens cap on at each of six settings, from ISO 200 at 1/4 s to ISO 1250 at 4 s, plus an evenly lit white frame. These are the settings where a full test of every ISO showed the most hot pixels. Each frame is judged on its own, because a pixel can look fine in four frames and jump in the fifth. The white frame catches pixels that are only wrong under light.
  3. Review. Each candidate is shown with a close-up, its coordinates and a plain-language reason. A pixel is ticked for you if it reads over 100 above its neighbours in every frame, or jumps over 200 in single frames at more than one setting. You can untick any of them, or tick a faint one by hand. Pixels at the very edge, and pixels the camera already lists but that still show, are reported but cannot be fixed here.
  4. Build. The new image is built offline. The tool checks that only the allowed tables changed, then runs it through an emulated copy of the camera's own firmware to prove the write path. That needs Fujifilm's firmware file, which you add once on the home screen; it stays on your computer.
  5. Write. You approve the exact image ID, put the camera into service mode, and the tool writes it. It then restarts the camera and reads the whole image back to prove the camera kept it.
  6. Check. Fresh dark frames are compared with the ones from the start. The report shows each pixel before and after, so you can see what actually changed on your camera.
Why the mechanical shutter? The guided run asks for the mechanical shutter (or Mechanical + Electronic), which is what the tool now tests with. Electronic frames on this camera are recorded with coarser steps and can show leaks that your normal photos would not. If a dark frame comes back electronic, the run stops and says so rather than mixing the two.

5 Why focus pixels are different

Some pixels on the sensor are not just for taking pictures: they are also used by the autofocus system to detect phase. They are called phase-detect or focus-sensor pixels. Adding one to the ordinary defect list is not the right fix, because the camera also needs that pixel's signal for focusing.

For a hot focus pixel, the tool instead changes one small correction table that controls how much of a focus pixel's own signal the camera trusts. Setting that value to zero stops the hot value from being passed on, while the surrounding focus information still works. Because the table covers every focus pixel at once, there is nothing to tick: the tool applies it automatically when it finds such a pixel.

The white frame is used as a sanity check here. Focus sites should read about the same as ordinary pixels under even light; if they do not, the report says so.

6 What it cannot do

  • It does not repair the sensor. It changes how the camera records the affected pixels. A physically damaged sensor, dust, or a cracked cover glass needs different work.
  • It cannot list every pixel. The camera's lists have a usable area, and a pixel at the very edge of the frame is reported but left alone.
  • It leaves twitchy pixels alone. At high ISO many pixels flare up for a moment and settle again, a different set each time, so a list cannot catch them. A pixel seen at only one setting is shown as faint and is not ticked by default; you can still choose to list one. Faint pixels under about 100 stay, too.
  • Its effect on fine detail is not proven yet. Running the raw correction at short speeds clears the bright pixels in dark frames, but it has not been checked on real photos. You can roll back at any time.
  • It is for one camera and firmware. This release is built and tested for the FUJIFILM X-T1 on firmware 5.52, on Linux. Other bodies and systems are planned, not supported yet.
  • It needs your approval. The tool never writes by itself, never writes a different image from the one you approved, and never assumes an interrupted write finished.

7 Technical details, for the curious

The exact places in the configuration image

The configuration image is 4,325,376 bytes (0x420000). The guided tool is allowed to change:

  • the pixel lists list0 at 0x76800 and list2 at 0x11c840;
  • the count fields from 0x28e0 to 0x2904;
  • the focus correction bytes 0x113b6 to 0x113d1 (28 bytes), set to zero only when needed;
  • the save bytes at 0x80 to 0xa0, which the camera's own save command writes.

Everything else is checked: image markers, the list-reload flag, the USB debug flag, the lens-profile block at 0x3e0000 to 0x3fffff, and every other byte. If anything else differs, the tool refuses to write. The full list is in the project's README.md.

What "read back" means

Writing to the camera is only half of it. After the write and a power cycle, the tool reads the entire image out again and compares it with what it sent. Only a full match counts as success. The read-back copy is saved as a locked backup too, and Roll back can put it back.

8 Words used in the app

Hot pixel
A pixel that reads too bright in the dark, usually more obvious at high ISO or in long exposures.
Dead pixel
A pixel that reads too dark, even under light.
Dark frame
A photo taken with the lens cap on. Any brightness in it is the camera's own leak, not light from the scene.
White frame
A photo of an evenly lit white screen. It shows pixels that are too bright or too dark only under light.
Defect list
The camera's own list of pixel coordinates that it replaces when saving a photo.
Focus pixel
A pixel also used by phase-detect autofocus. It gets the correction-table treatment instead of a list entry.
Pixel map
The configuration image holding the defect lists. "In camera" means a pixel map has been read from the camera and saved.
Read-back
Reading the whole image out of the camera after a write and comparing it with what was sent.
Roll back
Putting an earlier locked pixel map back into the camera, with the same approval and read-back checks.

This page explains the idea. The step-by-step procedure is in PROCEDURE.md, and the exact byte ranges and safety checks are in README.md and xpixelmap/README.md in the project folder.
